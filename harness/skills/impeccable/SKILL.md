description: Examine UI hierarchy, spacing, typography, interaction states and visual consistency against the supplied brief.

Use the supplied product requirements and reference UI as visual authority. Inspect their content, hierarchy, groups, surfaces and interaction states before changing a page. Make open design choices intentionally; do not substitute a generic landing-page treatment for an application workflow.

Check the built result, not merely the intended CSS:

- Group related controls tightly and separate unrelated sections; make hierarchy visible through alignment, spacing and type weight.
- Keep body and placeholder text readable, including on colored surfaces. Check contrast and keyboard focus against the actual background.
- Use the real copy at relevant viewport sizes; inspect overflow, wrapping and crowded controls.
- Include required hover, disabled, loading, error and empty states. Controls must work with pointer and keyboard.
- Keep actions, errors and recovery language consistent with the product's vocabulary.
- Use elevation, color, icons and motion to express meaning. Avoid unrelated decoration or a collection of identical containers that hides information hierarchy.
- Inspect the complete requested surface and its linked states, not only a pleasing first screen.

Batch observations that share a render to shorten feedback. Fix demonstrated defects and obtain evidence for the affected behavior. Do not impose a fixed number of inspection rounds or abandon an unresolved requirement to satisfy a polishing budget. Numeric styling habits are suggestions only when the brief leaves the decision open.
