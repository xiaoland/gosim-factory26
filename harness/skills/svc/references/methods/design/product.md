# Product Design

Use Product Design to turn an intended benefit into coherent behavior and interactions.
Understand the goal and current experience before extending a suggested feature or implementation idea.
Distinguish explicit requirements, observed use, and your own assumptions.

When there is no single correct answer, propose a concrete option and explain why it fits the goal better than a meaningful alternative.
Exercise judgment about usefulness, clarity, accessibility, recovery, and the cost of learning or changing the solution.
Keep the recommendation tied to its reasons, and revise it when those reasons no longer hold.
A convincing objection to the initial idea is useful work; an incomplete idea need not be accepted literally or abandoned.
Use prototypes, rendered alternatives, interaction replay, or direct observation when they reveal consequences more clearly than prose.

Make routine choices within the task's constraints and keep material assumptions visible and revisable.
Explain genuine conflicts or missing information that prevent a responsible choice.
Do not turn a provisional design choice into a claimed user requirement.
Carry the expected behavior and reasons into [Technical Design](technical.md) and [Test Design](test.md), so implementation and checks do not silently invent a different product.
