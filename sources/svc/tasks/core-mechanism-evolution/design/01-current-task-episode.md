## Why Use This Episode

This episode is not a benchmark and the task is not complete. It is useful
because:

- Sir's original outcomes and later corrections are explicit
- the Lead Agent's intermediate task structures remain inspectable
- the failure blocked discussion before code or durable product truth changed
- it spans local interaction quality and a longer ambiguous design trajectory

It cannot by itself establish prevalence, product outcome, causality, or a
universal SVC mechanism. It is not evidence for `S-SYSTEM`, and using its own
packet rewrite to prove that packet design would be circular. Later work needs
a dissimilar, non-self-referential Consumer episode with an observable terminal
result and cost boundary.

## Directly Observed Sequence

1. Sir requested foundational exploration for three purposes: more efficient
   Human-Agent collaboration with finer control and taste alignment; better
   Agent results on long ambiguous, numerous and interleaved work; and better
   results in large, complex, long-lived software development.
2. Sir clarified that “large software” means a very small Human team—most often
   one person, ordinarily up to approximately three to five—plus Coding Agents
   driving a large complex system.
3. The Lead Agent first created a compact two-file packet. Sir found it
   inadequate for the scale of the task, especially for discussion and design.
4. The Lead Agent expanded the packet into baseline, question map, decisions,
   verification, and a product-boundary dossier.
5. The expanded map organized future work around adaptive task control, Human
   control/taste, sub-agent orchestration, cognition/system design,
   large-system evolution, DX, and later experiments.
6. The first proposed decision replaced the three requested outcomes with
   “long-task recoverability,” “Human authority continuity,” and
   “system-change coherence,” then asked Sir to choose an outcome hierarchy.
7. Sir rejected both the substitutions and the design map, so substantive
   design discussion could not begin.
8. The Lead Agent withdrew that framing, reconstructed the original outcomes,
   and proposed the feedback inquiry topology accepted in `D-008`.

## Candidate Failure Chain

This is a hypothesis, not an accepted diagnosis:

```mermaid
flowchart LR
  U["Broad user outcomes"]
  A["Early abstraction into familiar SVC concerns"]
  P["Packet and question map stabilize those abstractions"]
  S["Evidence inquiries optimize inside the inherited frame"]
  D["Decision brief turns abstractions into forced choices"]
  H["Human detects category errors and stops discussion"]

  U --> A --> P --> S --> D --> H
```

The key possible failure is not merely lost information. The original outcomes
remained visible, but the Lead Agent may have treated familiar mechanisms as
better explanatory concepts and then mistaken the resulting consistency for
understanding.

## Competing Causal Explanations

| ID | Explanation | Supporting observation | What would weaken it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `C-01` | Premature abstraction and solution anchoring | The map used current SVC mechanism vocabulary before a reviewed outcome/causal model existed | Evidence that a neutral outcome model existed and the substitutions happened later for another reason |
| `C-02` | Missing representative Consumer episodes | Product language was derived from general goals and repository structure rather than a concrete success/failure trajectory | A concrete episode had already been supplied and was actively tested against the framing |
| `C-03` | Artifact-induced false confidence | More files, IDs, states, and a coherent DAG made the model easier to resume but not more faithful | The same forced choice would have appeared before the packet expansion |
| `C-04` | Delegated inquiry inherited a narrow question | Sub-agents critiqued packet structure and state but did not independently reconstruct the three product outcomes until after Sir's correction | An earlier delegated outcome audit already challenged the substitutions and the Lead ignored it |
| `C-05` | Evaluation and falsification arrived too late | No candidate wording was tested against “better task result,” “collaboration efficiency,” or “low-cost change” before decision options were offered | An explicit contradiction test was performed and passed under reasonable interpretations |
| `C-06` | Missing Human information was the primary cause | Terms such as “better,” “efficient,” and “low cost” were not operationalized | The error contradicted explicit wording strongly enough that clarification was not needed to avoid the substitutions |

Several explanations may coexist. `C-06` cannot excuse replacing an outcome
with a mechanism; it may explain why a faithful outcome remained underspecified.

## Candidate Better Trajectory

Before asking for a design decision, a better Lead-Agent trajectory may have:

1. preserved the three user outcomes verbatim as separate authorities
2. marked recovery, authority, coherence, packet structure, delegation, and
   cognition as hypotheses or candidate mechanisms
3. checked every abstraction with “what part of the requested result has this
   omitted?”
4. requested review of the outcome/causal model before constructing a design
   question graph
5. used the current episode or another Consumer case to test language and
   failure explanations
6. asked sub-agents to challenge the problem framing independently, not only
   improve the chosen packet structure
7. introduced decisions only after alternatives represented the same reviewed
   problem

These are expected behaviors under review, not approved SVC capabilities or
protocol rules.
