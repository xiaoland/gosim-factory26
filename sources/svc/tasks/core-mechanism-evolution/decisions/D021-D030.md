# Decisions D-021..D-030

## D-021 — Derive Primitives from Decomposition Dimensions Before Selection

- **State**: accepted
- **Authority and date**: Sir, 2026-08-05
- **Refines**: `D-017`; reopens the sufficiency part of `V-045`
- **Decision**: Do not accept `Slice` and `Step` as the only work-decomposition
  primitives yet. First derive candidate building blocks from breadth,
  refinement depth, concern continuity, temporal/control staging, integration,
  uncertainty/feedback, and authority/context boundaries.
- **Rationale**: A minimal vocabulary is harmful if one primitive absorbs
  several distinct completion, integration, ordering, or steering semantics.
  Conversely, adding approximate-size levels recreates ambiguity.
- **Consequences**:
  - Horizontal and vertical are starting lenses, not yet canonical terms,
    because both are overloaded in software work.
  - Candidate primitives are admitted by distinct semantic job and pressure,
    not by familiarity or an assumed hierarchy.
  - `Track` and a precisely bounded `Phase` return as candidates alongside
    `Slice` and `Step`; none is accepted durable vocabulary yet.
- **Reopen trigger**: Cross-case derivation shows that relations and qualifiers
  make additional primitives redundant without increasing Human inference
  cost, or another decomposition dimension is materially missing.

## D-022 — Planning Blocks Require Positive Net Management Value

- **State**: accepted
- **Authority and date**: Sir, 2026-08-05
- **Decision**: Every planning building block must provide sufficient net
  management value: compared with its absence, it lowers the total cost of
  understanding, steering, predicting, assigning, recovering, detecting
  conflict, or integrating the work by more than it costs to create, maintain,
  synchronize, and interpret.
- **Rationale**: A semantically non-empty definition can still create an empty
  management artifact. Task-packet structure exists to keep task completion
  under control, not to instantiate a complete ontology.
- **Consequences**:
  - Primitive admission requires an observable management decision or failure
    it improves, plus a no-block counterfactual.
  - A block activates only under sufficient pressure and retires when its
    continuing management job disappears.
  - File/module creation is a separate, higher-cost admission after the
    semantic block itself; a label, relation, or map row may suffice.
- **Reopen trigger**: A necessary shared semantic distinction cannot exhibit
  local cost reduction because its benefit appears only at a later scale, or
  the admission test consistently activates structure too late.

## D-023 — Semantic Derivation and Progressive Activation

- **State**: accepted
- **Authority and date**: Sir, 2026-08-05
- **Refines**: `D-017`, `D-021`, `D-022`
- **Decision**: Continue designing planning common ground by deriving distinct
  semantic jobs and progressively activating the smallest useful
  representation. Do not select primitives by approximate size, familiar
  hierarchy, or directory symmetry.
- **Rationale**: This preserves common language without making ordinary tasks
  carry the structure needed by rare complex tasks.
- **Consequences**:
  - The typed, optional building-block model is accepted as the inquiry model;
    the exact primitive names and set remain hypotheses.
  - Real packet counterfactuals compare control cost with and without each
    candidate before acceptance.
- **Reopen trigger**: Real use shows that progressive activation causes repeated
  restructuring or inconsistent Agent decisions whose cost exceeds a small
  fixed baseline structure.

## D-024 — Task Packet May Cache Selective Durable Truth and Candidate Deltas

- **State**: accepted
- **Authority and date**: Sir, 2026-08-05
- **Refines**: `D-018`, `D-022`; corrects the ownership risk in
  [`design/19`](../design/19-track-slice-counterfactual.md)
- **Decision**: A Track or other task-packet module may selectively restate
  product/unit-owned content as a task-local snapshot and may stage candidate
  durable changes before promotion. Repetition is not itself an ownership
  failure; it has management value when it creates a smaller local working set.
- **Rationale**: Repeatedly discovering and joining durable sources is costly,
  while task work often needs to reason over a contextual subset and proposed
  future state before durable truth can be updated.
- **Consequences**:
  - Distinguish relevant baseline snapshot, task-local working delta, and later
    promotion target when authority or freshness could otherwise be confused.
  - Do not require complete reproduction of durable owners; cache only what
    reduces current control cost.
  - On task closure, promote accepted truth through existing documentation
    ownership and allow volatile packet material to disappear.
- **Reopen trigger**: Snapshot provenance/promotion ceremony costs more than
  direct durable lookup, or stale cached truth repeatedly misdirects task work.

## D-025 — Promotion Targets Exist Only as Meaningful Planned Retrospective Work

- **State**: accepted
- **Authority and date**: Sir, 2026-08-05
- **Refines**: `D-024`
- **Decision**: Do not maintain promotion targets in Track, cache, or other
  non-Plan locations. Retrospective/consolidation discovers needed durable
  updates and represents them as planned semantic work; a target document path
  by itself has insufficient management value.
- **Rationale**: Premature target lists become stale bookkeeping and confuse
  likely file location with a reviewed truth change. Promotion is valuable only
  when the task knows what meaning should change and why.
- **Consequences**:
  - Track cache keeps relevant baseline and working delta, not a promotion
    checklist.
  - A promotion Plan item states the semantic change, evidence/decision basis,
    reconciliation need, and acceptance; file address is only supporting data.
- **Reopen trigger**: Repeated task closure loses accepted deltas because no
  lightweight earlier signal survives until retrospective.

## D-026 — Linear Plan May Stop Honestly at `to-be-continued`

- **State**: accepted
- **Authority and date**: Sir, 2026-08-05
- **Decision**: Plan is a linear but partial route. It may end at an explicit
  `to-be-continued` state when future work cannot yet be predicted honestly,
  with a coarse outline and continuation condition rather than invented steps.
- **Rationale**: Limited foresight makes false completeness more harmful than a
  bounded plan horizon. An honest stop improves steering and replanning.
- **Consequences**:
  - TBC is a Plan state at the current knowledge boundary, not a work item or a
    completion claim.
  - Record why planning stops and what evidence, decision, or return permits it
    to continue; a bare “later” has no recovery value.
- **Reopen trigger**: TBC becomes a habitual substitute for planning the next
  knowable action or creates more recovery ambiguity than a tentative route.

## D-027 — Task Plan Retires When Track or Phase Topology Activates

- **State**: accepted
- **Authority and date**: Sir, 2026-08-05
- **Refines**: `D-016`, `D-023`
- **Decision**: A small Task may own a Task Plan. Once Track or Phase is
  activated, the Task is no longer represented by one linear Plan; the root
  owns the Task topology/current fronts and linear Plans move to the applicable
  Track, Phase, or their intersection.
- **Rationale**: A global linear Plan misrepresents the non-linear topology that
  justified introducing Track or Phase and becomes a monolithic schedule.
- **Consequences**:
  - Root `Next Step` remains a coordination pointer, not proof that a Task Plan
    exists.
  - No competing global Plan duplicates local Plan state in a large packet.
- **Reopen trigger**: A short global critical-path projection measurably lowers
  Human control cost without becoming a second Plan or hiding parallel work.

## D-028 — Phase Instances May Have Declared Scope

- **State**: accepted
- **Authority and date**: Sir, 2026-08-05
- **Decision**: Phase uses common longitudinal semantics but may apply to a
  declared subset of Tracks; Task design cannot assume every Track progresses
  synchronously.
- **Rationale**: Real mixed tasks place Tracks at different maturity and control
  horizons. A Phase remains useful only when its exit predicate has a clear
  participating scope.
- **Consequences**:
  - Task-global Phase remains available when one true barrier covers all
    material Tracks.
  - The proposed `Track × Phase` Cell/barrier representation remains a
    hypothesis until separately reviewed.
- **Reopen trigger**: Scoped Phases create overlapping partial orders that cost
  more to understand than either Track-local Plans or one Task-global barrier.

## D-029 — Scoped Phase Exits Through Required Track/Phase Cells

- **State**: accepted
- **Authority and date**: Sir, 2026-08-05
- **Refines**: `D-028`
- **Decision**: Each Track participating in a scoped Phase contributes through
  an addressable `Track × Phase` Cell. Cells may progress asynchronously, but
  the Phase exits only after every required Cell satisfies the semantic Phase
  exit predicate. A Cell owns the local control projection and, by default, one
  current partial linear Plan.
- **Rationale**: Cell makes one Track's bounded Phase contribution judgeable
  without pretending the continuing Track itself completes at every Phase or
  requiring a global Task Plan.
- **Consequences**:
  - Prefer semantic addresses such as `Collection-P1`; shorthand such as `A1`
    requires a local legend.
  - Cell is a derived control unit and may begin as a map entry; no directory or
    file follows automatically.
  - `TBC`, blocked, or inactive required Cells do not satisfy Phase exit.
- **Reopen trigger**: Cell coordination costs more than direct Track-local
  Plans, or common exit meaning cannot be stated without duplicating detailed
  Slice/evidence state.

## D-030 — A Phase Requires a Real Shared Barrier

- **State**: accepted
- **Authority and date**: Sir, 2026-08-05
- **Refines**: `D-022`, `D-029`
- **Decision**: Do not create or preserve a Phase merely to make a Track/Phase
  matrix complete. A Phase is admitted only when later work should genuinely
  wait for a shared semantic/evidence/authority exit condition across its
  participating Tracks.
- **Rationale**: A decorative barrier introduces false synchronization, idle
  work, empty Cells, and misleading completion semantics instead of lowering
  control cost.
- **Consequences**:
  - If one Track can safely cross the proposed boundary without another, change
    the Phase scope/exit meaning or use separate scoped Phases.
  - Tracks outside the real barrier have no empty or `N/A` Cell.
  - Matrix symmetry has no admission value.
- **Reopen trigger**: A deliberately uniform barrier measurably lowers Human
  coordination cost despite allowing safe work to wait, and the cost is an
  accepted product trade-off.
