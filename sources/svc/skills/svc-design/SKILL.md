# Design

Use this skill when the intended behavior or its realization needs a consequential choice. Start with a representative journey or state transition, trace its owner and failure path, and challenge the proposal with a plausible counterexample.

For product behavior or conflicting original and derived requirements, read [product design](references/product.md) directly.
For ownership, interfaces, lifecycle, failure behavior, or a change in how an entry point is used, read [technical design](references/technical.md) directly.
Read [the design workflow](references/workflow.md) when the product and technical views must be made coherent, and [engineering judgment](references/engineering-judgment.md) when boundaries or complexity are growing; its [live-sync example](references/engineering-judgment.md#example-complexity-introduced-by-a-product-assumption) shows when to reconsider the product obligation before adding machinery.
Keep requirements, assumptions, reasons, alternatives, and residual uncertainty visible.
Use [check design](../svc-verification/references/check-design.md) to ground acceptance judgments and [evidence design](../svc-verification/references/evidence-design.md) to choose observations that can support or challenge the design.
Use [feedback and evolution](../svc-verification/references/feedback-and-evolution.md) when deciding how product exploration or implementation feedback should revise it.

Before a consequential interpretation, shared boundary, or uncertain acceptance criterion becomes a commitment, use [Independent Judgment](references/workflow.md#independent-judgment) to obtain and consume an independent perspective.

Use [project documentation](../svc-documentation/SKILL.md) to publish agreed project rules and shared contracts that other work must consume.
