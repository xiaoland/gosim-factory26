# Product Design

Use Product Design to turn an intended benefit into coherent behavior and interactions.
Understand the goal and current experience before extending a suggested feature or implementation idea.
Distinguish explicit requirements, observed use, and your own assumptions.
State the behavior that must remain stable, the behavior that is still exploratory, and the assumptions that implementation may change.

When dividing work, preserve the scope and conditions of the original requirement in each consumer's materials.
Read the relevant scenarios as well as their summary; parallel objects, named states, exceptions, and initial conditions can change the design.
Do not replace an explicit set of allowed and disallowed behaviors with a convenient ordering or a smaller representative subset.
A shared interpretation remains a design decision with a source and rationale, not a new authority that overrides the requirement.
When a derived work package contradicts its source, compare the actual statements and bring the material difference to the decision owner before propagating the derived version.
Narrowing a member's responsibility does not narrow the promised product behavior.
An intentional requirement change needs an explicit decision and updated affected materials; assignment alone does not establish that change.
If the original sources themselves conflict, preserve the alternatives and affected outcomes, choose an interpretation within the task's authority, and record its reasons and remaining uncertainty.
Continue unaffected work while resolving the disputed part rather than silently dropping it from the completion claim.

When there is no single correct answer, propose a concrete option and explain why it fits the goal better than a meaningful alternative.
Exercise judgment about usefulness, clarity, accessibility, recovery, and the cost of learning or changing the solution.
Keep the recommendation tied to its reasons, and revise it when those reasons no longer hold.
A convincing objection to the initial idea is useful work; an incomplete idea need not be accepted literally or abandoned.
Use prototypes, rendered alternatives, interaction replay, or direct observation when they reveal consequences more clearly than prose.

Make routine choices within the task's constraints and keep material assumptions visible and revisable.
Explain genuine conflicts or missing information that prevent a responsible choice.
Do not turn a provisional design choice into a claimed user requirement.
Carry the expected behavior, important state transitions, and reasons into Technical Design and the verification skill, so implementation and checks do not silently invent a different product.
When a behavior matters for acceptance, make it observable and repeatable enough for the intended evidence without prescribing an incidental route or storage mechanism.
